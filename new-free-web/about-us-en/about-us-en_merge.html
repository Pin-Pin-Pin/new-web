About Us

Citizen of the Earth, Taiwan (CET) is a local environmental protection foundation in Taiwan. Our team consists of environmental practitioners with more than twenty years of experience in Taiwan.

Our Vision

CET is dedicated to achieving environmental sustainability in Taiwan and tackling the challenges of climate change through direct action. Our mission is to raise awareness within Taiwanese civil society and inspire a willingness to take action. Ultimately, we aim to empower everyone to become 'Citizens of the Earth.'

Our Approach

We employ diverse strategies in our campaigns to protect the planet and promote civic participation in environmental decision-making. These range from non-institutional protests to institutional actions such as environmental litigation and policy advocacy.

Our actions are firmly rooted in investigating, researching, monitoring, and exposing environmental issues to develop and propose effective solutions. To achieve this, we collaborate closely with environmental organizations, local groups, residents, scholars, and experts.

To popularize environmental awareness, we actively publish editorials and books, host engaging events and lectures, and actively manage social media channels to foster direct conversations with the public.

Taiwan's Environmental Challenges & What We Do

Taiwan confronts the scars left on the land by inadequate legal frameworks and political-economic interests: from illegal factories on farmlands and outdated mining operations in mountains, to large-scale tourism developments and energy facilities on coasts and in forests that ignore local autonomy. Our goal is to end the reckless plundering of national land and rebuild a new order of land governance that respects ecological boundaries, social justice, and local sovereignty.

What we achieved

We have prevented tens of thousands of hectares of deforestation in Taiwan and preserved large areas of precious farmland, coasts, and wetlands.

In collaboration with NGOs and legislators, the Mining Act was finally amended in 2023, marking the most significant mining reform in Taiwan in 20 years. During the 7 years of pushing the act, we also facilitated the government's reclamation of 96 mining rights, covering a total area of 16,073 hectares.

Together with g0v, we developed the “Illegal Factory on Farmland Reporting System," which offers a user-friendly interface and allows anonymous reporting. With the system, we collected 1,546 cases and reported over 500 illegal factories on farmland, leading to the demolition of 11 illegal factories.

We reject the outdated narrative that development inevitably brings pollution, dedicating ourselves to dismantling the environmental burdens imposed by aging industries and industrial expansion. We monitor the environmental commitments of the petrochemical, steel, and semiconductor industries, exposing the hidden air, water, and social costs behind corporate profits. Through requiring the government to ensure information transparency, enforce strict regulations, enhance monitoring, and present accurate pollution data, we aim to transform the daily lives of those trapped in sacrifice zones. We strive to restore the right to a clean environment for communities long plagued by pollution, ensuring that corporations take on their rightful environmental and social responsibilities during industrial transitions, returning to all citizens the dignity of breathing and living with peace of mind.

What we achieved

We have successfully fought for the closure of CPC’s No. 5 Naphtha Cracker. We opposed the expansion of the No. 3 cracker in Linyuan and supervising it.

We stopped two coal-fired units at Dalin Power Plant and ended coal-burning at the aging Southeast Cement Plant.

We have also advocated for air quality index reform, air pollution caps, and reducing the transmission of electricity from south to north during periods of heavy air pollution.

We have exposed and monitored water pollution by companies such as Formosa Plastics and ASE Group in the Houjin River, and AU Optronics and Chunghwa Picture Tubes in the Shiao-Li River, successfully pushing for the amendment of regulatory laws.

We advocate that the energy transition must encompass more than mere technological shifts; it requires a profound social deliberation on democratic participation and ecological balance. While combatting the climate crisis and moving toward a net-zero future, we monitor the quality of renewable energy development, opposing chaotic solar deployments that sacrifice food security and wildlife habitats. By pushing for the legalization of rooftop solar, we bring energy back to everyday life. Simultaneously, we monitor generational safety following nuclear decommissioning and establish a transparent, accountable pathway for energy governance, ensuring that Taiwan's pursuit of energy autonomy upholds environmental justice and social democracy.

What we achieved

We facilitated the Environmental and Social Check Process for solar fish farms and the ecological compensation plan for the solar fish farm area in Tainan.

We prompted the Executive Yuan to increase the 2025 rooftop PV target from 6GW to 8GW and facilitated the regulation of rooftop PV proportions for new factories in industrial areas.

We contributed to the passage of the "Renewable Energy Development Act," which mandates the installation of solar panels in new, expanded, and renovated buildings.

We oppose development driven by short-term interests and safeguard the land's capacity for future choices. We intervene in major decisions made in the name of economic growth that could irreversibly restrict the living space of future generations—such as large-scale tourism developments, water resource disputes, and cultural heritage conflicts—to ensure that resources are not monopolized by specific interest groups. We defend the rights of local communities to participate in development decisions and deliberate on the fairness of resource allocation amid the challenges of climate change. This is a process of preserving a fair, diverse, and dignified living environment for the next generation, free from the erosion of contemporary hegemonic interests.

What we achieved

Together with society, we facilitated Taiwan’s 2025 plan for a low-carbon and nuclear-free energy transition and helped halt the construction of the Lungmen Nuclear Power Plant.

We have strived to integrate indigenous and local cultures and drive homeland and resource sustainability by influencing upper-level spatial planning and industrial systems.

Our Funding Resources

CET is Taiwan’s first environmental protection foundation established through public fundraising. To remain impartial and independent, we are funded through individual donations and do not accept government funding and donations from the corporations we supervise.
